# Those Eyes – New West

## Introduction

In this blog, I'm writing about the song _Those Eyes_ by the Canadian musical duo New West, released
in 2019. This song was suggested to me by my wife, who felt its simplicity reflected the love we
have for each other. The lyrics are uncomplicated and the mood is intimate — but while the song is
straightforward, it carries an important sentiment that people often forget as their relationship
matures.

## Background

_Those Eyes_ was the first song New West wrote together. Initially released in 2019 as a single, it
went viral on TikTok where it became synonymous with romantic and nostalgic content, with the
hashtag #thoseeyes amassing over 800 million views. The song even reached Southeast Asia,
demonstrating its universal emotional appeal. Part of what made it so resonant was its departure
from the "toxic" love tropes common in modern pop, in favor of a steady and quiet appreciation.

## The Song

### Verse 1

> When we're out in the crowd<br>Laughin' loud and nobody knows why<br>When we're lost at the
> club<br>Gettin' drunk and you give me that smile<br>Goin' home in the back of a car<br>And your
> hand touches mine<br>When we're done makin' love<br>And you look up and give me those eyes

The first verse opens with the couple in public, lost in their own world. There's an intimate
connection between them — laughing about an inside joke that nobody else understands. The setting
shifts from an open street to a club, then to the back of a car, and finally to somewhere private,
but their bond remains constant throughout.

This verse establishes the feeling of "us against the world." To an outside observer, these are just
small moments — but to the two people living them, it's everything.

### Chorus

> 'Cause all of the small things that you do<br>Are what remind me why I fell for you<br>And when
> we're apart and I'm missin' you<br>I close my eyes and all I see is you<br>And the small things
> you do

The chorus is the emotional anchor of the song. It turns inward, moving from external experience to
internal feeling. The emphasis on "small things" suggests that lasting love isn't built on grand
gestures, but on the everyday habits and expressions shared between two people — and it's those same
small things that keep you connected even when you're apart.

### Verse 2

> When you call me at night<br>While you're out gettin' high with your friends<br>Every "hi", every
> "bye"<br>Every "I love you" you've ever said

Verse 2 mirrors the structure of the first — grounding us in an external event before revealing the
emotional weight behind it. The simple act of calling when you're out with friends, and the routine
exchanges of "hi", "bye", and "I love you", carry so much significance precisely because they come
from the right person.

### Bridge

> When we're done makin' love<br>And you look up and give me those eyes

Before the final chorus, an instrumental bridge gives the listener space to feel the full sentiment
of the song before its most vulnerable line. It's in moments like these that you want to close your
eyes, hold onto your happiest memories, and simply exist.

## Final Thoughts

The song isn't complex — there are no hidden meanings or grand revelations — but therein lies its
beauty. These are the emotions we feel during the honeymoon phase of a relationship, when the
smallest gestures carry the greatest weight and we notice every little detail about each other. It's
during this time that we build the foundation of a relationship, and it's those small memories that
hold us together even when we're physically apart.

All the small things we do are a quiet language of love — simple and instinctive. Love shouldn't
require too much thought or planning. It's not about grand trips or expensive gifts. It's in a touch
of the hand, an "I miss you", or just the look in someone's eyes.
