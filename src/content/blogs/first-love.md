# First Love – Utada Hikaru

## Introduction

In this blog, I'm going to be writing about the song _First Love_ by Utada Hikaru, released in 1999.
I first heard this song probably sometime around high school and then was re-introduced to it when
it was covered by Le Sserafim's Kim Chaewon
([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rboiHxBqdZk)). Its lyrics are fairly simple, and the
general mood is melancholic. However, the combination of Hikaru's voice, and singing techniques with
its blend of Western R&B with simple strings and piano created a truly groundbreaking piece of
music. It defined a new era of J-pop and is still popular decades later.

## Background

Prior to releasing her debut album, _First Love_, Utada had already been working as a musician since
the age of 12. However, her early works did not see much success. A year before releasing _First
Love_, she had released an English R&B album that also failed to gain traction. The album failed to
release in the States at the time, but it did manage to catch the attention of a producer in Japan;
this gave her the opportunity to continue pursuing her musical career there. So Utada decided to
move full-time to Japan, leaving her life in New York behind. Her time in New York did however shape
her lyricism going forward, as she was able to utilize both English and Japanese in her songs. This
gave them a more conversational vibe and usually made them more blunt and raw compared to other
Japanese artists at the time, whose lyrics were usually more poetic in nature.

Another aspect of her life that shaped her lyrical maturity was the tumultuous relationship between
her parents. It's well known that her parents divorced multiple times, and this led to Utada growing
up in a constant cycle of separation and reconciliation. This would have heavily impacted her views
on love which may have led to the bittersweet underlying tones in _First Love_, as well as explain
the maturity of the lyrics, given that she wrote the song when she was only 15.

## The Song

Since I'm not a native Japanese speaker, I've done the breakdown as best I can using translation
apps and explanations from AI. I'll include the English translation beside each line.

Here's the full translation from
[genius.com](https://genius.com/Genius-english-translations-hikaru-utada-first-love-english-translation-lyrics).

### Verse 1

> _Saigo no kisu wa_ — Our last kiss _Tabako no kuni ga shita_ — Tasted like cigarettes _Nigakute
> setsunai kaori_ — A bitter and painful scent

In the first verse, she sets the bittersweet mood by evoking a memory of a last kiss and the
lingering scents and taste that accompany it. In Japanese culture, scent and taste are closely
linked to memory. An important element here is the word _Setsunai_. While it translates to
"painful," it's meant to portray a heart-constricting, suffocating feeling of helplessness. By
linking it with the scent of cigarettes, it's almost like the memory is choking her, because when
people smell cigarettes, it generally evokes the sensation of coughing or a tightness in the chest.

### Pre-chorus 1

> _Ashita no imagoro ni wa_ — By this time tomorrow _Anata wa doko ni iru n darou_ — I wonder where
> you are _Dare wo omotteru n darou_ — I wonder who you're thinking of

Here in the first pre-chorus, we can see her wondering about the current state of the other person,
and if they are in a different place (both emotionally and physically). There's a sense of anxiety
and uncertainty which will contrast with the second pre-chorus.

### Chorus

> You are always gonna be my love _Itsu ka dare ka to mata koi ni ochite mo_ — Even if I fall in
> love with someone else someday I'll remember to love You taught me how You are always gonna be the
> one _Ima wa mada kanashii love song_ — Right now, it's still a sad love song _Atarashii uta utaeru
> made_ — Until I can sing a new song

The chorus is where there are the most transitions between English and Japanese. This allows her to
express raw emotions without the filtering that exists in Japanese, which is known for its social
politeness. Additionally, phrases such as "I love you" are often seen as overly heavy and "cheesy"
in Japanese, but saying them in English acts as a buffer to make the declarations lighter and more
modern.

The chorus conveys how her first love will always hold that special place regardless of what
happens, and the lasting impact they had on her. There is also a sign of hopefulness: even though
she is sad right now, there's hope for her to find something new in the future. In Japanese culture,
the concept of _Hatsukoi_, meaning First Love, is seen as something sacred and unrepeatable, which
is why it's considered to have a profound impact on someone's life. This is why she emphasizes
remembering the loss and the lessons she's learned, treating the heartbreak with purpose rather than
viewing it as just a loss.

One notable metaphor in the chorus involves the use of "Song." In Japanese poetry, "singing a song"
is often used as a metaphor for how one lives their life. In a way, she's saying she's currently
stuck in this sad version of herself, until she can find a new chapter.

### Verse 2

> _Tachidomaru jikan ga_ — The time that has stopped _Ugokidasou to shite iru_ — Is trying to start
> moving again _Wasuretakunai koto bakari_ — There are only things I don't want to forget

This verse, following the chorus, carries a connotation of moving on and finding a new chapter.
Within the context of Utada herself, this could symbolize her moving from New York to Japan and the
transition she's experiencing. _Ugokidasou_, meaning time starting to move, often implies a change
in environment or a fresh start. While she is physically moving on, there are things, memories, that
she is mentally fighting to keep "stopped" in place.

### Pre-chorus 2

In the second pre-chorus, we can see that the focus has shifted more to first-person. Now it's a
form of introspection, and it becomes clear that she is predicting her own grief. Instead of
ambiguous words like _Doko_ (where) and _Dare_ (who), she uses words like _Kitto_ (surely) to convey
certainty. The two contrasting pre-choruses help create a sense of time where we go from
denial/curiosity to acceptance/despair.

One other interesting detail is the use of the word _Darou_ (I guess/probably). While she uses the
word in both pre-choruses, in the first it feels like a genuine question. However, the second use
sounds more like a resigned prophecy that she will not be okay because she knows herself.

### Bridge

> You will always be inside my heart _Itsumo anata dake no basho ga aru kara_ — Because there will
> always be a place just for you I hope that I have a place in your heart too Now and forever you
> are still the one _Ima wa mada kanashii love song_ — Right now, it's still a sad love song
> _Atarashii uta utaeru made_ — Until I can sing a new song

The bridge serves as a turning point in the piece and is intended to provide a different perspective
from the preceding verses and chorus. Here she grounds the imagery by creating a physical
architecture for her love through the word _Basho_ (place). Moreover, instead of only wallowing in
her own despair, she reaches out to the other person hoping that she will have a place in their
heart as well, just as she has made one for them.

### Chorus 2

> You are always gonna be my love _Itsu ka dare ka to mata koi ni ochite mo_ — Even if I fall in
> love with someone else someday I'll remember to love You taught me how You are always gonna be the
> one _Mada kanashii love song_ — Still a sad love song Now and forever...

Chorus 2 is very similar to the first chorus, except the last two lines are different. Instead of
saying that she will be singing a sad love song until she sings a new one, it's now been replaced
with "Now and forever". Repeating this line from the bridge seems to potentially suggest that the
First Love and the sad love song are tied together forever and have become inseparable.

Since "Now and forever" immediately follows the line "Still a sad love song", it could be
interpreted as:

- The person will always be the "one" now and forever, which, while sad, is hopeful because it could
  symbolize her ability to move onward
- The pain from the love has trapped her permanently in the "sad love song" such that now she's
  frozen in time and sadness

I think it's more of the latter, given that earlier in the song the chorus mentions "Until I can
sing a new song" but now, revisiting the past, she has dropped the "until" and replaced it with
"still". This creates a moment of resignation. She's realizing that she won't be able to find a "new
song" because the pain from the first one has left a permanent mark on her.

## Photographs

Below are some photographs I took to try to convey the emotions and meanings I gleaned from the
song. I also chose to shoot only in JPEG instead of RAW. I wanted to keep things simple and
worry-free, just like how first loves are.

### Sunset during sunrise

![Sunset during sunrise](/images/blog/first-love/web/sunset-during-sunrise.jpg)

This photo was taken sometime after sunrise, but I wanted to make it look and feel like a sunset. In
a way, a first love feels exciting like a sunrise, which leads to the start of a new day and looking
forward to what it will bring. The day is filled with endless possibilities, and it seems like we
can accomplish anything. Yet when the sun sets, we're sometimes hit with the realization that we
accomplished nothing in the end; all we're left with is regret and sadness that another day has
passed, and we can never get it back. These feelings are like when a first love ends. There's only
regret, sadness, and a realization that it's gone forever.

### Numero Uno Bench

![Numero Uno Bench](/images/blog/first-love/web/numero-uno-bench.jpg)

The bench was something I randomly stumbled upon. It was in a park, and I felt it fit perfectly with
what I wanted to capture: the words and the lonesome bench. Life is full of little coincidences and
often we don't take enough time to pause or feel gratitude for what we have now, instead always
pondering what we could have. Sometimes we should just take a seat and enjoy the moment.

### Unbridgeable Finite Distance

![Unbridgeable Finite Distance](/images/blog/first-love/web/unbridgeable-finite-distance.jpg)

Often, happiness feels like it's right within our grasp. We reach out wanting to feel the warmth and
the safety it provides, but sometimes that last step we want to take seems like an uncrossable
distance. This is one of the most prevalent feelings I have when listening to Utada's song. The
relentless struggle of wanting to move on, wanting to reach out, wanting to get back, but ultimately
failing to do any of these things. The memories that linger from your First Love are the only
reprieve in the absolute darkness that is despair, and we can't help but turn to them and try to
reminisce and find some comfort. However, this is exactly the trap that locks us in an eternal cycle
of trying to bridge the unbridgeable gap.

### Time has passed

Within Utada's song, there is a constant cycle of hope and despair, and while trapped in this cycle,
it feels like time has stopped moving. And yet, reality is cruel yet merciful. With time, the pain
will subside, and we'll carry on. The constructs we created to trap ourselves will crumble and the
sun will rise again. I wanted to capture a bit of that hope within this picture; two people who've
gone through life's tribulations but still hold on to each other dearly. They've gone through
heartbreak, loss, and sorrow but still believe in each other and the hope of tomorrow.

### Transition

![Bridge](/images/blog/first-love/web/bridge.jpg)

In Japanese culture, the bridge symbolizes the transitional journey from our physical plane to a
higher spiritual one. With that in mind, I hoped to capture that transition through our physical
life in this picture. The two people are meant to symbolize those who go through life with you. It
could be your partner, your friends or even your first love. Then, as life progresses and you reach
the end of the bridge, there is only a single person left. That person represents you now at the end
of your journey, alone. When it's your time to step off the bridge, you will do so alone, in
totality. Just like Utada said, it'll be a sad love song, now and forever.

---

### Extra Thoughts

With these photographs, I hoped to capture both the positive and negative emotions I felt from
Utada's song. While the predominant emotions it evokes are regret and longing, it's also bittersweet
— which is basically a reflection of life. People go through life piling up regrets, and there's an
ongoing battle between happiness and gloom. We like to reminisce about the good times in the past
and fail to live in the now or look to the future. Each of us is trapped in our own thoughts and
constructs, trying to always reach for something that feels just beyond our grasp, but may be
unattainable. Instead, take a seat on a bench and let the time pass. Slow things down and think
about what you have now and what you're grateful for. Because everything eventually ends, and at the
end, there'll be just one song, the song of your life, now and forever.
